# Engagement ring glossary (for prompts and catalogue wording)

- Status: DRAFT by Peter, 2026-11-10. **The owner checks it.** Lines marked `[OWNER CHECK]` are
  where makers use different meanings or where NORE's own meaning matters. Until the owner has
  checked it, NORE's meaning is unconfirmed.
- Use: Peter's prompts and Freddie's catalogue lines. For Gemini (one-shot, every call billed),
  **never rely on the term alone.** Gemini knows the common meaning, which may not be NORE's.
  Write the term plus where the part sits and what it looks like (the "Prompt wording" column),
  and name the parts that must stay plain.
- The ring, top to bottom: **centre stone** (table, crown, girdle, pavilion, culet), held by
  **prongs** on the **head**. The head's frame is the **basket**, made of **gallery** rails, with
  the **bridge** under the stone. The head sits on the **shoulders** (sometimes **cathedral**
  arches), which run into the **shank** (band).

## The centre stone

| Term | Where / what | Prompt wording (for Gemini) | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Table | The large flat facet on top of the stone | "the stone's flat top (table) faces up" | Gemini tilts the table to the camera unless told |
| Crown | The faceted upper part of the stone, between the table and the girdle | "the faceted upper part (crown)" | rarely needed in prompts |
| Girdle | The thin outer edge at the stone's widest point, where the prongs grip | "the stone's outer edge (girdle)" | the prong tips meet the girdle |
| Pavilion | The faceted lower part, under the girdle, tapering to a point | "the lower, pointed part of the stone (pavilion), inside the head" | in a side view it shows through the gallery |
| Culet | The very bottom tip of the pavilion (a point, or a tiny flat facet) | "the bottom point of the stone (culet)" | must not touch the finger; sits just above the shank |

## The head (what holds the stone)

| Term | Where / what | Prompt wording | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | The whole assembly on top of the ring that holds the centre stone: prongs, basket, gallery, bridge | "the head (the metal setting that holds the centre stone)" | |
| Basket | The open frame of the head under the stone: prongs joined by one or more horizontal rails, like a little cage | "a basket head: the prongs joined by a thin metal rail below the stone" | NORE's builder uses "Basket" for the prong choice `[OWNER CHECK]` |
| Gallery | The side profile of the head under the stone (the rails and openings you see from the side); also the open space under the stone | "the gallery (the side of the head under the stone, seen in profile)" | Gemini fills it with decoration if it isn't described |
| Bridge (under-gallery rail) | The curved metal rail directly under the centre stone that joins the prongs, seen from the side. On NORE's marked photo it's the arc under the stone. **Not the cathedral arches, not the shoulders.** | "the bridge: the narrow curved metal rail directly under the centre stone that joins the prongs, seen from the side" | Owner-confirmed meaning (via Freddie, 2026-11-10). Never use "bridge" for the arches in a prompt. |
| Prongs / claws | The metal posts that rise from the head and grip the stone's girdle | Use one word per prompt: **"prongs"** | Mixing "claws" and "prongs" may add parts (run 20/21 hypothesis) |
| Prong tip shapes | Round (ball), claw (pointed), flat, tab | "round prong tips" | `[OWNER CHECK]` which tips NORE uses |
| V-prong (chevron) | A V-shaped prong that covers and protects a point (pear tip, marquise ends, princess corners) | "one V-shaped prong covering the pointed tip" | counts as **one** prong |
| Double claw (double prong) | Two thin prongs side by side at each position instead of one | "a pair of thin prongs at each position" | the opposite of "each prong is one single claw" |
| Split prong | One prong that divides into two tips at the top | "each prong splits into two tips at the top" | Gemini confuses it with the double claw |
| Tulip setting | A head whose prongs curve out and up like tulip petals around the stone | "a tulip head: the prongs curve outward like the petals of a tulip, holding the stone" | `[OWNER CHECK]` is this one of NORE's 7 settings? |
| Bezel | A thin metal rim that fully (or partly) circles the stone's girdle instead of prongs | "a bezel: a thin smooth metal rim all around the stone's edge" | not prongs; no prong count |
| Hidden halo | A ring of small stones round the head **under** the centre stone, at the girdle or just below, seen from the side and not from above | "a hidden halo: a ring of small stones around the head just under the centre stone, visible from the side, not from above" | Gemini turns it into a normal halo; say "not visible from above" |
| Peekaboo stones | Small stones set where they only show from certain angles, e.g. in the sides of the head, the gallery or the shank sides | "small stones set into the side of [PART], only seen from the side" | close to bridge stones `[OWNER CHECK]` whether NORE uses this name |

## Shoulders and shank (band)

| Term | Where / what | Prompt wording | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shank | The band: the whole ring of metal round the finger | "the band (shank)" | |
| Shoulders | The parts of the shank on each side that rise up to meet the head | "the shoulders (where the band rises to meet the head)" | Gemini puts "stones on the bridge" here unless the bridge is defined |
| Cathedral | The shoulders sweep up in arches on each side to lift and support the head, like the arches of a cathedral | "cathedral shoulders: the band sweeps up in one smooth, solid arch on each side to hold the head" | NORE's builder calls this a "Bridge" choice `[OWNER CHECK]`; in prompts say "shoulders", never "bridge" |
| Split shank | The band divides into two strands as it nears the head | "the band divides into two strands on each side near the head" | never write it in a plain-band prompt except as an avoid line |
| Twisted / crossover band | Two strands that wrap around each other | "two strands twisting around each other" | |
| Comfort fit | The inside of the band is rounded (domed), not flat, for comfort | "the inside of the band is rounded (comfort fit)" | barely visible in 3/4 and top renders, so leave it out of image prompts |
| Profile | The shape of the band's cross-section: flat, domed, knife-edge | "a domed band" | |

## Stone settings on the band and around the head

| Term | Where / what | Prompt wording | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pavé | Many small stones set close together on the surface, held by tiny beads of metal, so it looks paved with stones | "a single row of small stones set close together along the top of the band (pavé)" | say where the row starts and stops |
| Micro-pavé | The same as pavé with even smaller stones (well under 1 mm) and tinier beads | "very small stones in a tight row (micro-pavé)" | Gemini blurs these into glitter; ask for crisp individual stones |
| Channel set | Small stones set in a groove between two metal walls, with no beads | "small stones held between two metal walls (channel set)" | |
| Halo | A ring of small stones around the centre stone, at the top, visible from above | "a halo: one ring of small stones around the centre stone, visible from above" | |
| Side stones / three-stone | Larger stones set beside the centre stone | "one [SHAPE] side stone on each side of the centre stone" | |

## Metal decoration

| Term | Where / what | Prompt wording | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milgrain | A row of tiny beads along a metal edge | "a fine beaded edge (milgrain) along [PART]" | Gemini adds it to vintage-looking rings; list it as an avoid unless chosen |
| Filigree | Fine, lace-like openwork patterns in the metal, often on the gallery or shoulders | "lace-like openwork (filigree) on [PART]" | |
| Engraving | Lines or text cut into the metal | — | text in an image prompt is unreliable; NORE renders carry no text |
| Two-tone | Two metal colours on one ring. Often the head (basket and prongs) is white metal on a coloured band | "the prongs holding the centre stone are bright white gold; everything else is [METAL]" | runs 20/21: extra prongs added on the pear and marquise |

## Gemini notes (from NORE tests, as reported by Freddie; not owner-confirmed)

- Every view is a one-shot, billed call. Write the prompt so it passes on the first try, and
  never plan a second render (owner's direction, 2026-11-10).
- A name alone isn't enough: Gemini uses the common meaning (e.g. "stones on the bridge" became
  pavé shoulders 6 of 6 times when "bridge" was the arches).
- Keep the camera line second (proven in runs 7–8).
- Say each fact once. Repetition grew the prompt to 1,148 words without making it more accurate.
