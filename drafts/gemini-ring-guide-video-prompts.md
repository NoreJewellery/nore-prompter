## Method, and why

1. Make one still picture of the ring (Prompt 1) and approve it.
2. Make a second still of the same ring from the side (Prompt 2), with picture 1 attached, and
   approve it.
3. Make one short clip per part (Prompt 3). Every clip starts on an approved still and only moves
   the camera in. Seven clips, in the owner's order.

Why not one long video: what Peter found on 2026-11-13 suggests a Gemini clip runs about 8 to 10
seconds, which is too short for seven parts. Why every clip starts on an approved still: it is the
strongest way to keep the ring identical, and the clips then join cleanly. Playing a clip backwards
gives the pull-back to the whole ring.

These limits came from a web search, mostly from third-party pages, and are not confirmed against
Google's own pages: clip length about 8 to 10 seconds; a picture can be used as the first frame;
720p made directly, with 1080p or 4K by upscaling on some plans.

Resolution is a setting in the app, not something a prompt can change. Choose the highest quality
the app offers, and use "upscale" if it is offered.

## Before approving a picture, check

- exactly four claws, one at each cropped corner
- the stone is clearly longer than wide, and points along the finger
- a row of small diamonds just under the stone's edge (the hidden halo), and no halo on top
- the bridge is plain metal
- the band is thin, and the pavé stops about three quarters of the way round
- nothing extra: no side stones, no second band, no text

## Prompt 1: the first picture (3/4 view)

```text
Photorealistic studio product picture of ONE engagement ring. Nothing else in the picture.

The ring
- Metal: polished white gold.
- Centre stone: one colourless diamond, radiant cut: an elongated rectangle with cropped corners, clearly longer than it is wide. Its length runs along the finger, at a right angle to the band, not across the finger. It is a large 4 carat stone, much wider than the thin band.
- Prongs: exactly four claw prongs, one at each cropped corner of the stone. Each claw tip rests on the stone.
- Hidden halo: one single row of small round diamonds set into the metal rail just under the stone's edge, running all the way round the head. It is seen from the side, never from straight above. There is no halo around the top of the stone.
- Bridge: a plain polished bar of metal at the base of the head, just above the finger opening. No stones on it.
- Shoulders: cathedral. On each side the band rises in a smooth arch to meet the head.
- Band: thin and delicate, under 2 mm wide, the same width all the way round. Pavé band: one row of small diamonds set along the top of the band, running about three quarters of the way round; the part of the band under the finger is plain polished metal.
- No side stones, no second band, no engraving, no milgrain.

Camera: 3/4 view. The ring stands upright on its band, seen from the front and a little to one side, with the camera slightly above. The whole ring is in the frame with space around it, sharp from front to back.
Background: plain warm cream (#f4e7c9), with a soft shadow under the ring. Soft, even studio light.
No text, no logo, no hand, no box, no props.
Square 1:1 picture, at the highest resolution available.
```

## Prompt 3: one clip per part (attach the still named in the table)

The first clip, in full:

```text
Use the attached picture as the first frame of the video. It shows the one ring this video is about.

Make one continuous shot with no cuts. The ring does not move, turn or change in any way. Only the camera moves.

Camera: start on the attached picture exactly as it is and hold for one second. Then glide slowly and smoothly forward until the part named below fills about half of the frame. Hold still on it for the last two seconds. Do not travel round to another side of the ring.

Keep the ring identical to the attached picture in every frame: the same elongated radiant centre stone, the same four claw prongs at its corners, the same single row of small diamonds under the stone's edge, the same plain bridge, the same thin white gold band with its single row of pavé diamonds. Do not add, remove or reshape any stone, prong or piece of metal.

Keep the plain warm cream background and the soft, even studio light unchanged. Sharp focus on the part the camera moves to. Realistic macro product film.
No text, no labels, no logo, no hand, no props, no other jewellery. No sound and no music.
Square 1:1 frame. If square is not available, use a wide 16:9 frame and keep the ring inside the central square at all times.

Move in to: the centre stone, the elongated radiant diamond, so that its top face and cropped corners are clear.
```

## What is most likely to go wrong

- Picture 2 is the risky step. Gemini has to work out the underside from picture 1, and it may
  change the claw count or the band. If picture 2 is wrong, redo picture 2, not picture 1.
- In a clip, the hidden halo and the four claws are the details most likely to drift. A clip
  where the ring changes is a retry of that one clip only.
- A first try with untested wording is a test, not a likely pass. Expect retries.
- A sharp still with the zoom done in the builder's own code would keep the ring identical with
  no video at all. That is Freddie's call if the clips keep drifting.

On the website the result is a concept render, not a photo of a finished ring. The "Concept
render" label goes on the page, not inside the picture or video.
